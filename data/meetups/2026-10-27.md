    description:
      zh: |
        大部分求職產品都在試圖回答同一個問題：「如何幫助使用者更快找到工作？」
        但如果使用者已經努力了幾個月，甚至超過一年，依然沒有得到理想的結果呢？

        Slope 是一款結合遊戲化思維與黑色幽默的求職追蹤產品。靈感來自希臘神話中不斷推石頭上山的薛西弗斯（Sisyphus），用一種帶點荒謬的方式，回應現代求職者不斷投遞履歷、等待回覆、重新開始的日常。

        Slope 並不試圖將求職包裝成一場快樂的遊戲，也不假裝每一次努力都會得到回報。

        相反地，它希望透過進度視覺化、適度的遊戲化回饋，以及帶點自嘲的黑色幽默，為使用者提供一點情緒上的支持。

        對於求職時間長達一年以上的使用者，Slope 也探索如何借用遊戲中的 Campaign（戰役）概念，將漫長、零散的求職歷程整理成可以回顧的階段與里程碑。無論這段旅程是個人選擇，還是受到現實環境所迫，過去付出的努力都不應該因為尚未找到工作而被一筆勾銷。

        這次分享將從遊戲設計的角度，探討如何在結果充滿不確定性的情境中，運用遊戲化與幽默，設計出更有同理心的產品體驗。
      en: |
        Most job search products focus on one question: "How can we help people find a job faster?"

        But what happens when someone has been trying for months, or even over a year, without getting the results they hoped for?

        Slope is a job tracking product that combines gamification and dark humor to support job seekers through the emotional challenges of a long job search.

        Inspired by the myth of Sisyphus, endlessly pushing a rock uphill, Slope uses a slightly absurd perspective to reflect the reality of applying, waiting, and starting over again.

        The goal isn't to turn job hunting into a fun game or pretend that every effort leads to success. Instead, Slope explores how visible progress, small moments of recognition, and a little dark humor can offer emotional support when external rewards are missing.

        For people searching for a job for over a year, Slope also explores the idea of treating the journey like a game campaign, organizing months of scattered effort into chapters, milestones, and a history of progress. Whether the long journey is a personal choice or something beyond their control, their effort shouldn't disappear simply because they haven't reached the final goal.

        In this session, Sean will share how game design thinking can help create more empathetic product experiences, especially when success is uncertain and motivation is difficult to maintain.
